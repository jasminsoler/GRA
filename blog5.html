Face Masks 101

How to Choose One for Your Skin’s Needs

A simple guide to choosing hydrating, clay, soothing, and exfoliating masks without turning your routine into a 12-step experiment.

There is something about a face mask that instantly makes skincare feel a little more special. Maybe it is the five minutes you finally take for yourself. Maybe it is the ritual of applying something creamy, cooling, or refreshing while your favorite playlist is on.

But once you start looking at all the different masks available, things can get confusing. Clay masks. Sheet masks. Gel masks. Cream masks. Exfoliating masks. Overnight masks.

So, how do you choose a face mask? The easiest approach is to start with what your skin needs right now—not what is trending or what sounds the most luxurious.

A hydrating mask can make sense when your skin feels dry or tight. A clay-based mask may be useful when you want to absorb excess oil. A soothing mask can be a better choice when your skin feels easily irritated. And exfoliating masks can help remove surface buildup when used appropriately.

How Do You Choose a Face Mask?

Choose a face mask based on your skin type, current skin needs, and the ingredients in the formula. Look for hydrating masks when skin feels dry, clay-based masks when you want oil-absorbing care, soothing formulas when skin feels sensitive, and exfoliating masks when you want gentle surface exfoliation.

You do not need to use a mask every day—or use multiple masks at once—to have a thoughtful routine.

01

THE EXTRA STEP

What Does a Face Mask Actually Do?

A face mask is a skincare treatment designed to sit on the skin for a specific amount of time before being removed—or, in some cases, left on overnight.

Depending on the formula, a mask may be designed to add hydration, help absorb excess oil, gently exfoliate the skin, provide a soothing skincare step, support a more comfortable-feeling skin barrier, or make your routine feel more intentional.

Face masks are usually supplemental skincare—not a replacement for your everyday routine. Your cleanser, moisturizer, and sunscreen generally play a much more consistent role in a basic routine.

Think of a mask as the little extra. The skincare equivalent of putting on your favorite robe after a long day.

MASK MATCH

What Does Your Skin Need Right Now?

01
HYDRATION

If Your Skin Feels Dry or Tight

Look for a hydrating or moisturizing mask. These formulas are generally designed to support hydration and leave skin feeling softer and more comfortable.

Common ingredients can include hyaluronic acid, glycerin, aloe, and ceramides. A humectant helps attract and hold water in the outer layers of the skin.

02
OIL CONTROL

If Your Skin Tends to Feel Oily

A clay-based mask may be a better fit. Clay can absorb oil from the skin’s surface. Common types include kaolin and bentonite.

But more oil absorption is not automatically better. If your skin feels uncomfortably tight afterward, the formula or frequency may not be working well for you.

03
SOOTHING

If Your Skin Feels Sensitive

Look for a gentle, soothing mask with a simple formula. If your skin is already irritated, stinging, peeling, or unusually reactive, it may be better to skip the mask.

04
EXFOLIATION

If Your Skin Feels Dull or Rough

You may be interested in an exfoliating mask. The key is moderation, especially if you already use exfoliating acids, retinoids, or other active treatments.

THE ROUTINE

How Often Should You Use a Face Mask?

There is no universal number of times everyone should use a face mask. The right frequency depends on the mask, its ingredients, your skin’s tolerance, and the rest of your routine.

Always follow the product’s instructions first. A gentle hydrating mask may be appropriate more frequently than a strong exfoliating treatment.

If your routine already contains several active ingredients, keep your mask routine especially simple.

More skincare does not automatically mean better skincare. Consistency usually matters more than constantly adding new steps.

05

ORDER OF OPERATIONS

Before or After Your Skincare Routine?

Generally, a wash-off mask goes after cleansing and before the rest of your leave-on skincare.

Cleanse → Face Mask → Serum or Treatment → Moisturizer

If you are using an exfoliating mask, consider what other active products you use that day. For sheet or hydrating masks, follow the product’s specific directions.

The instructions on the specific product matter.

KEEP IT SIMPLE

Do You Need More Than One Face Mask?

Not necessarily. You do not need an entire collection of masks to have a good skincare routine. One well-chosen mask that fits your needs may be more useful than a shelf full of products you rarely use.

Some people like having different options depending on the day: hydrating for dry-feeling skin, clay for an oilier-feeling day, soothing for self-care night, or exfoliating when it fits the rest of the routine.

The important part is paying attention to what your skin needs rather than forcing your skin into a rigid schedule.

THE SKIN KISS MOMENT

Make Masking a Ritual.

The best skincare rituals are not necessarily the ones with the most steps. Sometimes, it is simply cleansing your face, applying a treatment, putting your phone down for a few minutes, and letting yourself slow down.

That is the Skin Kiss approach: skincare can be effective and enjoyable. If a face mask is your little weekly ritual, make it feel like one. Set aside a few minutes, keep your tools clean, choose products intentionally, and resist the urge to turn one mask into a 12-step experiment.

Questions We Get Asked

What is the best face mask for your skin?

There is no single best face mask for everyone. The right choice depends on your skin type, current needs, and the rest of your routine.

How often should you use a face mask?

It depends on the product and your skin’s tolerance. Always follow the product’s instructions.

Can I use a face mask every day?

You generally do not need to. Many routines benefit from occasional treatments rather than daily masking.

Should I wash my face before using a face mask?

Usually, yes. Cleansing first helps remove makeup, sunscreen, oil, and everyday buildup.

Can I use a face mask after exfoliating?

A gentle hydrating mask may be appropriate, but combining multiple exfoliating treatments can increase the risk of irritation.

Do face masks replace moisturizer?

No. Masks are generally an optional treatment, while moisturizer remains a regular skincare step for many routines.

How long should I leave a face mask on?

Follow the instructions for the specific product. Leaving a mask on longer does not necessarily make it more effective.
