Skincare Tools 101: What They Do & How to Use Them

New to skincare tools? Here's your guide to facial rollers, gua sha, cleansing tools, cooling tools, LED devices, and how to use them gently.

What Do Skincare Tools Actually Do?

Skincare tools are accessories or devices designed to support specific parts of a skincare routine, such as cleansing, facial massage, cooling, or at-home treatments. Different tools serve different purposes, so how you use one depends on the tool. Most should be used gently, kept clean, and treated as an addition to—not a replacement for—a consistent skincare routine.

What Are Skincare Tools?

Skincare tools are reusable accessories or devices designed to complement your skincare routine.

Unlike a cleanser, moisturizer, or serum, a tool doesn't necessarily deliver skincare ingredients to your skin. Instead, it may help you cleanse, massage, cool, or otherwise enhance a particular part of your routine.

Common examples include:

  • Facial rollers
  • Gua sha tools
  • Facial cleansing brushes
  • Silicone cleansing tools
  • Cooling globes
  • Under-eye massage tools
  • LED skincare devices
  • Facial spatulas or applicators

And here's the important part:

Not every skincare tool is meant to do the same thing.

A cleansing brush and a facial roller belong in completely different parts of your routine. Understanding the purpose of each tool is more useful than simply collecting whatever happens to be trending.

Facial Rollers

A facial roller is a handheld tool with one or two rolling heads designed to glide across the skin.

They're commonly used for gentle facial massage and cooling , and many people enjoy incorporating them into a relaxing morning or evening ritual.

How do you use a facial roller?

Start with clean skin and use gentle pressure.

  1. Cleanse your face.
  2. Apply a lightweight skincare product if you'd like additional slip.
  3. Begin around the center of your face.
  4. Gently roll outward toward the cheeks and hairline.
  5. Move upward along the jawline and cheeks.
  6. Use light pressure around the eye area if the tool is designed for it.
  7. Clean the roller after use.
More pressure does not mean better results.

A facial roller should feel comfortable—not painful, scratchy, or irritating.

02

Gua Sha

Gua sha is a traditional technique involving a smooth-edged tool that is moved across the skin.

In modern skincare routines, facial gua sha is often used as a form of facial massage .

How do you use gua sha?

The key is slip and gentle pressure .

  1. Start with clean skin.
  2. Apply a product that allows the tool to glide comfortably.
  3. Hold the tool relatively flat against your skin rather than digging its edge into the face.
  4. Use slow, gentle strokes.
  5. Work from the center of the face outward.
  6. Avoid aggressively scraping or repeatedly dragging over one area.
  7. Clean the tool after use.

Gua sha shouldn't be painful or leave you feeling like you've given your face a workout.

The goal of a skincare ritual is not to make your skin angry.
03

Facial Cleansing Brushes

Cleansing brushes are designed to help physically cleanse the skin.

Some have soft bristles, while others use silicone surfaces that vibrate or move across the face.

Are facial cleansing brushes good for your skin?

They can be, but they aren't necessary for everyone.

The main consideration is how your skin responds to them.

Using a cleansing tool too aggressively or too frequently can contribute to irritation, especially if your skin is already sensitive or you're simultaneously using exfoliating products.

If you use one, keep the pressure light and follow the manufacturer's instructions.

Think: gentle cleansing—not scrubbing.

If your skin feels tight, raw, unusually sensitive, or irritated afterward, that's a sign to reconsider how you're using the tool.

04

Silicone Cleansing Tools

Silicone cleansing tools are another option for adding a little extra texture to your cleansing routine.

They're generally easy to rinse and clean, which makes hygiene an important part of their appeal.

How do you use a silicone cleansing tool?

Use it with your regular cleanser:

  1. Wet your face.
  2. Apply cleanser.
  3. Gently move the tool across the skin.
  4. Avoid pressing hard.
  5. Rinse your face thoroughly.
  6. Clean and dry the tool.

There's no need to spend several minutes scrubbing your face.

Your skin should feel clean and comfortable , not squeaky or stripped.

05

Cooling Globes & Under-Eye Tools

Cooling tools are popular for one simple reason:

They feel really good.

These tools are commonly used as part of a soothing facial massage ritual, particularly around areas like the cheeks and under-eyes.

The cooling sensation can make your routine feel refreshing, especially in the morning.

Just remember that the under-eye area is delicate.

Use light pressure and avoid dragging or pulling the skin.

06

LED Skincare Devices

LED skincare devices use specific wavelengths of light and are a little different from a simple massage tool.

Some at-home LED devices are designed around specific skincare concerns, but their effectiveness depends on the device, the light used, how it is used, and the specific concern being addressed.

If you're considering an LED device, follow the manufacturer's directions carefully.

More is definitely not better.

Don't assume that using a device longer or more frequently will automatically improve results.

And if you have a skin condition, take photosensitizing medications, or have concerns about whether an LED device is appropriate for you, it's worth checking with a healthcare professional before using one.

Do You Need Skincare Tools?

No. Skincare tools are optional.

A simple routine can absolutely be effective without them.

Your basic routine may already include:

  • Cleanser
  • Moisturizer
  • Sunscreen

With additional products such as serums or treatments depending on your individual needs.

Tools can make the experience more enjoyable, add a relaxing ritual, or serve a specific purpose—but they shouldn't make skincare feel like homework.

If a tool makes your routine more complicated than it needs to be, skip it.

When Should You Use Skincare Tools?

It depends entirely on the tool.

  • Facial roller or gua sha
    Usually used after cleansing, often alongside a product that provides enough slip for comfortable massage.
  • Cleansing brush
    Used during cleansing, according to the tool's instructions.
  • Silicone cleansing tool
    Used with cleanser.
  • Cooling tool
    Can generally be incorporated into a morning or evening ritual, depending on the product instructions.
  • LED device
    Follow the manufacturer's specific directions for timing, frequency, eye protection, and skin preparation.
The tool determines the timing—not the other way around.

Should You Use a Skincare Tool Before or After Your Products?

There isn't one universal answer.

A cleansing tool belongs during cleansing. A facial massage tool is typically used after cleansing and should have enough slip to glide comfortably.

An LED device should be used according to its specific instructions, including whether it should be applied to clean, dry skin or used alongside particular products.

The important thing is to avoid forcing every tool into the same routine step.

Your skincare routine should have a purpose— not just more steps.

How Often Should You Use Skincare Tools?

Follow the instructions for the specific tool and pay attention to how your skin responds.

A gentle facial massage tool may be comfortable to incorporate regularly, while an exfoliating or cleansing device may require more moderation.

When you're introducing a new tool, start conservatively rather than immediately using it as often as possible.

And if you're already using active skincare ingredients—such as retinoids or exfoliating acids—be especially mindful of adding tools that create additional physical stimulation.

Your skin doesn't get bonus points for doing the most.

Common Skincare Tool Mistakes to Avoid

A beautiful tool can still be a bad addition to your routine if you aren't using it properly.

MISTAKE 01

Using too much pressure

Facial tools should generally glide across the skin rather than tug, scrape, or cause pain.

MISTAKE 02

Using dirty tools

A tool that touches your face needs to be cleaned regularly. Follow the manufacturer's cleaning instructions, particularly for electronic devices.

MISTAKE 03

Using tools on irritated skin

If your skin is already feeling raw, uncomfortable, or irritated, adding friction may not be the best move.

MISTAKE 04

Using every tool at once

You don't need a seven-step tool routine. Pick tools based on what you actually enjoy and what makes sense for your skincare goals.

MISTAKE 05

Expecting instant transformations

A skincare tool can complement a routine, but it shouldn't be treated as a shortcut to perfect skin. Healthy-looking skin is usually much more about consistency than having the fanciest vanity setup.

How Do You Keep Skincare Tools Clean?

Clean your skincare tools after use according to the manufacturer's instructions.

This is one of the least glamorous parts of owning skincare tools—and one of the most important.

  • Rinse away product residue when appropriate.
  • Use the recommended cleanser or cleaning method.
  • Dry the tool completely.
  • Store it somewhere clean and dry.
  • Don't share personal facial tools.
  • Follow specific care instructions for electronic devices.

If a tool has cracks, damaged surfaces, worn bristles, or other areas that are difficult to clean, it's worth replacing it.

Your tools should be clean, too. ♡

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Skincare Tools FAQ

Are skincare tools actually worth it?

They can be worth it if they add something useful or enjoyable to your routine. They are not required for healthy-looking skin, and the value depends on the tool, how you use it, and whether it fits your habits.

Can I use a facial roller every day?

A gentle facial roller may fit comfortably into a regular routine, but there is no universal need to use one daily. Use light pressure, follow the tool's instructions, and reduce use if your skin becomes uncomfortable or irritated.

Should I use gua sha before or after skincare products?

Gua sha is generally used after cleansing with enough product slip to allow the tool to move comfortably. The exact product and technique can vary, so follow the tool's instructions and avoid aggressive pressure.

Can skincare tools help with puffy-looking skin?

Cooling or massage tools can feel refreshing and may temporarily change the appearance or sensation of puffiness. They should not be treated as a permanent solution for swelling or an underlying medical concern.

How often should I clean my skincare tools?

Clean reusable facial tools after use or according to the manufacturer's instructions. Remove residue, dry the tool completely, and store it somewhere clean and dry. Electronic devices should be cleaned according to their specific care instructions.

Are facial cleansing brushes good for sensitive skin?

They may not be appropriate for everyone with sensitive skin. Physical stimulation can contribute to irritation, especially when used aggressively or alongside exfoliating products. If you try one, use it gently and follow the manufacturer's directions.

Do I need skincare tools for a good skincare routine?

No. A simple routine can be effective without skincare tools. Cleanser, moisturizer, sunscreen, and products selected for your individual needs can form the foundation of a routine; tools are optional additions.
