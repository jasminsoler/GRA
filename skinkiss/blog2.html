Skincare Tools 101

What They Do & How to Use Them

From facial rollers and gua sha to cleansing tools and cooling globes, a beginner-friendly guide to what skincare tools do, how to use them, and what not to expect from them.

THE BEAUTY DESK01

Skincare has officially entered its little ritual era. From facial rollers and gua sha to cleansing tools and cooling globes, there seems to be a beauty tool for just about every step of your routine.

And honestly? We love a pretty vanity moment.

But once you get past the gorgeous packaging and dreamy bathroom-counter aesthetic, a very reasonable question comes up: What do skincare tools actually do?

More importantly, do you really need them?

The short answer is no—skincare tools aren't essential for maintaining a basic skincare routine. A good cleanser, moisturizer, and sunscreen are still the foundation.

But the right tool can be a lovely addition to your routine. Some are designed to help cleanse the skin, while others are used for facial massage, temporary cooling, or simply turning a few everyday minutes into a more intentional ritual.

Let's get into it.

QUICK ANSWER

What Are Skincare Tools?

Skincare tools are handheld beauty accessories designed to complement parts of your skincare routine, such as cleansing, facial massage, cooling, or product application.

Examples include facial rollers, gua sha tools, cleansing brushes, and cooling globes. They are optional additions—not replacements for basic skincare products or professional dermatological care.

THE FOUNDATION

Do You Actually Need Skincare Tools?

No. Skincare tools are optional.

Your skin doesn't require a jade roller, gua sha tool, or cleansing brush to be healthy-looking and cared for.

The fundamentals still matter most:

  • Gentle cleansing
  • Moisturizing
  • Daily sun protection
  • Products appropriate for your individual needs
  • Consistency

So why use a tool at all? Because skincare isn't only about the products you apply.

For some people, tools make the routine feel more enjoyable and intentional. A few minutes of facial massage can become a relaxing part of a nighttime ritual. A cooling tool may feel refreshing in the morning. A cleansing accessory can make washing your face feel a little more elevated.

The best skincare tool is one that adds something useful—or enjoyable—to a routine you already have.

01

FACIAL ROLLERS

Roll With It

A facial roller is a handheld tool with one or more smooth rolling surfaces designed to glide across the face. They're commonly used for facial massage and can provide a cooling, soothing-feeling ritual when used gently.

HOW TO USE IT

  1. Start with clean skin and a clean tool.
  2. Apply a skincare product that provides enough slip for comfortable movement, if appropriate for your routine.
  3. Place the roller gently against your skin.
  4. Roll outward and upward with light pressure.
  5. Move across the cheeks, jawline, forehead, and other areas as comfortable.
  6. Clean the tool after use according to the manufacturer's instructions.

The important word here is gentle. You don't need to press hard to make a facial roller “work.”

02

GUA SHA

Think Massage, Not Scraping

Gua sha is a traditional technique that has been practiced in various forms across East and Southeast Asian cultures. Modern facial gua sha tools are typically smooth, flat tools used for gentle facial massage.

HOW TO USE IT

  1. Start with clean skin.
  2. Apply a suitable facial oil or product that allows the tool to glide without pulling.
  3. Hold the tool relatively flat against the skin.
  4. Use gentle, slow strokes.
  5. Work in sections rather than repeatedly dragging the tool over one area.
  6. Clean the tool after use.

Think massage, not scraping. If you're applying enough pressure to cause significant discomfort, that's a sign to ease up.

03

CLEANSING TOOLS

Keep the Cleanse Gentle

Cleansing tools are designed to assist with washing the face. Depending on the design, they may feature silicone bristles, textured surfaces, or other materials intended to help distribute cleanser across the skin.

HOW TO USE IT

  1. Wet your face with lukewarm water.
  2. Apply your cleanser.
  3. Use the tool gently over the skin.
  4. Avoid aggressive scrubbing.
  5. Rinse thoroughly.
  6. Clean and dry the tool after use.

More friction does not automatically mean cleaner skin. If your skin feels irritated, overly tight, or uncomfortable afterward, reduce the pressure or frequency—or stop using the tool.

04

COOLING TOOLS

A Refreshing Little Chill

Cooling skincare tools are designed to provide a chilled sensation during your routine. They're often used in the morning or whenever you want a refreshing facial ritual.

GOOD TO KNOW

Cooling tools can temporarily make skin feel refreshed and can help reduce the sensation of warmth or puffiness for some people. However, don't confuse a temporary cosmetic effect with a permanent change in your skin.

You don't need to freeze your face to get the benefit. Use a comfortably chilled tool and avoid prolonged exposure to extreme cold.

ROUTINE ORDER

How Do You Use Skincare Tools in Your Routine?

Use skincare tools after cleansing and around the appropriate point in your routine based on the tool and product you're using.

For a simple facial massage ritual, you might follow:

Cleanse → Apply a product that provides slip → Use tool → Continue with your routine

The exact order can vary. A cleansing tool belongs during your cleansing step—not after you've finished your skincare routine.

A facial massage tool may be more comfortable after applying a suitable oil or other product that allows it to glide without pulling.

Always follow the specific instructions that come with your tool.

BEFORE OR AFTER?

Should You Use a Skincare Tool Before or After Skincare Products?

It depends on the tool. There isn't one universal rule for every skincare tool.

Cleansing tools
Use them with your cleanser during the cleansing step.

Facial rollers
They can generally be used during a facial massage step, often after cleansing and with a product that allows comfortable movement.

Gua sha
Typically used with a facial oil or another suitable product that provides enough slip to minimize pulling.

Cooling tools
Can generally be incorporated into a routine when desired, as long as the tool is used safely and according to its instructions.

Don't force every tool into the same skincare routine.

The tool should fit the ritual—not the other way around.

KEEP EXPECTATIONS REAL

Can Skincare Tools Replace Skincare Products?

No.

A facial roller doesn't replace moisturizer.

A gua sha tool doesn't replace sunscreen.

A cleansing tool doesn't replace a cleanser.

And a cooling tool doesn't replace whatever targeted skincare products you may use.

Think of tools as accessories to your routine, rather than the foundation.

Your products take care of the skincare basics. Your tools can help make the experience feel a little more intentional.

THE DON'TS

Skincare Tool Mistakes to Avoid

A beautiful tool still needs to be used thoughtfully.

01

Using too much pressure

Your face isn't a countertop. Keep movements gentle, especially when using tools designed for massage.

02

Using tools on irritated skin

If your skin is already feeling irritated, adding friction may make the situation worse. Give your skin a chance to settle.

03

Forgetting to clean your tools

Tools come into direct contact with your skin, so cleanliness matters. Clean them according to the manufacturer's instructions and allow them to dry properly.

04

Sharing facial tools

Your skincare tools are personal items. Avoid sharing them with others.

05

Using a tool simply because it's trending

TikTok can introduce you to some genuinely fun beauty discoveries—but a viral tool doesn't automatically belong in your routine.

Ask: What is this tool supposed to do? Do I actually want that benefit? Will I enjoy using it?

07

SAFETY FIRST

Are Skincare Tools Safe for Everyone?

Not necessarily. Most skincare tools are designed for cosmetic use, but individual skin can respond differently.

If your skin is irritated, injured, sunburned, or unusually sensitive, it may be better to skip tools that involve friction, pressure, or massage until your skin has recovered.

If you have a diagnosed skin condition, recently had a cosmetic procedure, or aren't sure whether a particular tool is appropriate for you, consider asking a qualified healthcare professional before using it.

This article provides general skincare information and isn't a substitute for personalized medical advice.

Questions We Get Asked

A few skincare-tool questions worth keeping bookmarked.

01What are skincare tools used for?

Skincare tools are used to complement different parts of a beauty routine. Depending on the tool, they may assist with cleansing, facial massage, cooling, or creating a more intentional skincare ritual. Facial rollers and gua sha tools are commonly used for massage, while cleansing tools are designed to assist with washing the face. Skincare tools are optional and should complement—not replace—basic skincare products.

02Are skincare tools actually worth it?

Skincare tools can be worth it if they provide an experience or function you genuinely value. They aren't necessary for a basic skincare routine, and results can vary depending on the tool and individual. For many people, the appeal is the ritual itself: a few extra minutes of facial massage, cleansing, or cooling can make skincare feel more enjoyable and intentional.

03How often should I use a facial roller?

There is no universal frequency that applies to every facial roller. If your skin tolerates gentle facial massage comfortably, you may choose to incorporate a roller regularly according to the tool's instructions. Use light pressure and avoid repeatedly rolling over irritated or damaged skin. Clean your roller regularly, and stop using it if it causes discomfort or persistent irritation.

04Is gua sha good for your skin?

Gua sha can be incorporated into a facial massage routine and may provide a relaxing, soothing-feeling experience when performed gently. However, it shouldn't be treated as a cure for skin concerns or as a replacement for skincare products. Use a clean tool, apply enough product for comfortable movement, and avoid excessive pressure. If your skin is irritated or recently treated, consult a healthcare professional before using it.

05Should I use a facial roller before or after moisturizer?

It depends on the type of roller and the rest of your routine. Many people use facial rollers with a product that provides enough slip for comfortable movement, which may be a serum, facial oil, or another suitable skincare product. Follow the manufacturer's instructions and avoid rolling aggressively over dry skin if doing so causes pulling or discomfort.

06Can skincare tools cause irritation?

Yes. Skincare tools can cause irritation when they are used too aggressively, too frequently, or on already irritated skin. Friction and pressure can be particularly uncomfortable for sensitive or compromised skin. Use gentle movements, keep tools clean, and follow the manufacturer's directions. If you experience persistent redness, burning, pain, or irritation, stop using the tool.

07Do I need skincare tools in my routine?

No. Skincare tools are optional. A simple skincare routine can be built around foundational products such as cleanser, moisturizer, and sunscreen. Tools are best thought of as additions that can complement your routine, provide a particular cosmetic experience, or make your skincare ritual more enjoyable. Choose them because they serve a purpose—not because you feel like you need every trending beauty tool.
